\section{Executive Summary}

This report provides a preliminary review of the Weather Enhancement Technology Array (WETA)
installed by Rain Enhancement Technologies (RET) on the Flat Iron Mesa, outside Moab, Utah.
WETA is a self-sufficient and ``off grid'' solution installed to provide enhancement of precipitation
in year-round operations (snow and rainfall) over the La Sal Mountain Ranges. the focus of this report is on the February 2026 operating period.

\subsection{Operational Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report covers 28 days from 02/01/2026 to 02/28/2026.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
    \item WETA operated on nine days in January 2026.
    \item Precipitation was observed on seven of those days.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item Like December 2025, January 2026 was a climatologically dry month.
  \item Snow events on 1--2 January, 8--9 January, and 23--24 January replenished some of the snowpack that had degraded through December.
  \item The evidence presented in this report supports the likelihood that precipitation was enhanced by WETA over the La Sal range.
  \item Radiometrics radiometer data is being processed. Through consultation with Dr.\ Binod Pokharel, radiometer-derived quantities will be incorporated in forthcoming analyses as covariates.
  \item Power issues related to the radiometer elaborated in the previous report were addressed in early February.
  \item Addition of load shedding and backup generator for improved power reliability on site completed in early February.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actions and Improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item January precipitation, snow pack depth, and snow-water equivalent were evaluated against observations (both current and historical) from the nearby Abajo Mountains. Expectations for untreated snowpack were deduced through analysis of snowpack in the La Sal Mountains compared to the Abajo in past years with similarly dry conditions.
  \item Installation of further instrumentation in La Sal Ranges (planned Mar/Apr 2026).
  \item Treatment probability maps are being calculated using HySPLIT simulations and estimated cell drift. A full event-based catalog of spatially-varying treatment probabilities will be presented in the next reporting cycle.
  \item WETA operations will continue to target predicted precipitation events that are not considered adverse weather conditions or impacting snow pack.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of Snow Pack and Snow-Water Content Enhancement}

Snow pack enhancement was evident in the target area. The nearby Abajo mountains to the south of La Sal offer a means of establishing a control. We compared snow depth, snow-water equivalent, and accumulated precipitation at pairs of SNOTEL sites in the La Sal and Abajo ranges. Specifically, we compared observations from La sal Upper to Buckboard Flat, and La sal Lower to Camp Jackson. Figure \ref{fig:snow} presents snow-water equivalent content at these pairs of sites, respectively. The comparison presented in Figure \ref{fig:snow} reveals the following:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The climatology of snow water content at La sal Upper is similar to that at Buckboard Flat. La sal Lower maintains, on average, a shallower snowpack with less water content than Camp Jackson.
  \item All sites had much less water in snowpack than average in December 2025 and January 2026 (see red circles in Fig.\,\ref{fig:snow}).
  \item Despite the dry conditions, the La Sal sites maintained a deeper snowpack with more water content than the Abajo sites. The differences were above the climatological mean.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Establishment of Controls: Comparison of February 2026 to Analog Years}

While Figure \ref{fig:snow} indicates that enhancement was likely, quantitative estimation of the enhancement requires controls that are appropriate for the historically dry conditions that were observed in February 2026. We therefore evaluated the February 2026 observations against analogy years that were exceptionally dry. Using precipitation accumulation as a basis for selection, the five driest years since 2013 were 2020, 2015, 2016, 2014, 2013. The differences between observations in the La Sal and Abajo ranges during these years were used to estimate ``expected differences''. Differences observed in 2026 that exceeded those expected differences are a measure of ``enhancement above expectation''. Table \ref{fig:enhancement} summarizes the expected enhancement values for February 2026 using this methodology. At La sal Upper, snow depth and snow-water content may show enhancement above the dry-year expectation; the comparison between La sal Lower and Camp Jackson is subject to more variability, most likely due to the lower altitude and proximity to snowline.

\subsection{Analysis of Radiometer Data}

Radiometer-estimated liquid water content indicated significant differences in cloud liquid water over the La Sal range during the 23--24 January snow event. Fig.\,\ref{fig:radiometer} presents the difference in vertically-integrated liquid water between two azimuthal directions -- one to the northeast directed towards the La Sal range, and one directed vertically. Higher values over the La Sal range were evident during the snowfall period on the 23rd (e.g., see Fig.\,\ref{fig:precip}). RET has consulted with Dr.\ Binod Pokharel on best use of the radiometer data. Based on that discussion, derived radiometer quantities will be used as covariates to evaluate whether LWC or supercooled LWC may moderate the efficacy of enhancement.
