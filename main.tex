\section{Executive Summary}

This report provides a preliminary review of the Weather Enhancement Technology Array (WETA)
installed by Rain Enhancement Technologies (RET) on the Flat Iron Mesa, outside Moab, Utah.
WETA is a self-sufficient and ``off grid'' solution installed to provide enhancement of precipitation
in year-round operations (snow and rainfall) over the La Sal Mountain Ranges. The focus of this report is on the May 2026 operating period.

\subsection{Operational Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report covers 31 days from 05/01/2026 to 05/31/2026.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
    \item WETA operated on twenty-six days in May 2026.
    \item Precipitation was observed on six days in May.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item Several events produced snow at the highest elevations in the La Sal (e.g., at the La Sal Upper SNOTEL at 9580~ft) but only minimal snow at lower altitude (e.g., at the La Sal Lower SNOTEL at 8780~ft).
  \item Given the warming conditions, the large gradient in snowfall at high elevation, and the loss of snowpack at observation sites, this report will focus on liquid or liquid-equivalent precipitation.
  \item Resolution of previously-reported power issues permitted longer continuous periods of operation, including a stretch beginning on 4/20 that continued into early May and a second stretch beginning on 5/16.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actions and Improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report features an analysis of precipitation using the same methods employed during the cold season, including a comparison between observations in the La Sal Mountains and the nearby Abajo mountains.
  \item Installation of further instrumentation in La Sal Ranges is planned this summer.
  \item Precipitation observed at additional existing gauge sites inside and outside of the target area will be incorporated into the next monthly report.
  \item Methods of evaluation for warm-season precipitation are being tested presently, including incorporation of data from the Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS) dataset.
  \item WETA operations will continue to target predicted precipitation events that are not considered adverse weather conditions.
  \item A method for enacting automatic shut-off during lightning events near WETA is currently in construction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of Precipitation Enhancement}

Figure \ref{fig:snow} presents box-and-whisker plots summarizing the snow-water equivalent (SWE) across all cold-season months at SNOTEL sites in the La Sal target area and in Abajo Mountains control area. The evident loss of snowpack in March, April, and May obviates the need for continued snowpack analysis.

Figure \ref{fig:precip_month_clim} presents the total accumulated precipitation observed in the storage gauges at the same SNOTEL locations that have been used to monitor snowpack. As in previous reports, the analysis focuses on pairs of highly-correlated sites in the La Sal and Abajo ranges (i.e., La Sal Upper and Buckboard Flat, La Sal Lower and Camp Jackson).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The total precipitation at all sites in May 2026 (red circles in Figure \ref{fig:precip_month_clim}) was above the climatological mean.
  \item The difference in total precipitation was higher than average at La Sal Upper compared to Buckboard Flat. However the difference was slightly lower than average at La Sal Lower compared to Camp Jackson.
  \item The discrepancy is likely due to the small sample size of events and the higher degree of precipitation variance intrinsic to warm-season precipitation.
  \item The noisy nature of warm-season precipitation underscores the need to incorporate additional observations over a longer period of record.
\end{enumerate}
