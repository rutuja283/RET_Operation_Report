\section{Executive Summary}

This report provides a preliminary review of the Weather Enhancement Technology Array (WETA)
installed by Rain Enhancement Technologies (RET) on the Flat Iron Mesa, outside Moab, Utah.
WETA is a self-sufficient and ``off grid'' solution installed to provide enhancement of precipitation,
including snowfall during the cold season and rainfall during the warm season. For this report, the
warm season refers to the April--July operating period, with the present report focused specifically
on July 2026.

\subsection{Operational Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report covers 31 days from 07/01/2026 to 07/31/2026.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
    \item WETA operated on twenty-four days in July 2026.
    \item Precipitation was observed on eighteen days in July at one or more La Sal SNOTEL sites.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item July 2026 was substantially wetter than June across the region. Positive target-minus-control
  differences were obtained at La Sal Upper and Gold Basin relative to Buckboard Flat, while
  La Sal Lower was slightly below Camp Jackson. Sample size and convective spatial variability
  still limit definitive attribution for a single month.
  \item Given the absence of snowpack at the observation sites, this report focuses on liquid
  precipitation during the July operating period.
  \item The longest continuous operating period for WETA within July was 11 days, between
  07/11/2026 and 07/21/2026. A later continuous period beginning 07/24/2026 extended into
  early August.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actions and Improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report evaluates warm-season precipitation using methods consistent with those applied
  during the cold season, including comparisons between observations in the La Sal Mountains
  and selected reference stations from the NRCS SNOTEL network. Candidate stations were
  assessed based on historical correlation with the La Sal target sites, geographic relationship
  to the treatment area, elevation and climatic setting, and consistency of the available
  precipitation records. Primary controls are Abajo Mountains SNOTEL pairs
  (Buckboard Flat and Camp Jackson).
  \item Installation of additional instrumentation in the La Sal Mountains is planned for this summer,
  pending the necessary USDA Forest Service permitting and approvals.
  \item Methods of evaluation for warm-season precipitation are being tested presently, including
  incorporation of data from the Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS) dataset and secondary
  consistency checks against NWS Cooperative Observer Program (COOP) stations.
  \item WETA operations will continue to target predicted precipitation events that are not considered
  adverse weather conditions.
  \item An automatic shut-off capability for lightning events occurring within a 6-mile radius of WETA
  is currently under development as a protection system for the WETA.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of Precipitation Enhancement}
\label{sec:evaluation}
 
Enhancement is evaluated by comparing accumulated precipitation at target sites in the La Sal
Mountains against control sites outside the treatment area, using records from the SNOTEL
locations that have supported this program since its inception. SNOTEL precipitation is measured
with an all-season storage gauge \citep{fleming2023}, which reports year-round and is therefore
suitable for warm-season (April--July; hereafter ``warm season'') rainfall analysis. Candidate
control sites were drawn from two observing networks (NRCS SNOTEL and NWS COOP) and assessed on
three joint criteria: correlation of April--July monthly precipitation totals with the target,
consistency of instrumentation with the target gauges, and geographic position relative to the
treatment area.
 
The Abajo Mountains provide the primary controls. Buckboard Flat (SNOTEL 1153) and Camp Jackson
(SNOTEL 383) are among the most strongly correlated candidates for every La Sal target over the
warm season ($r = 0.81$--$0.85$ for the pairs adopted here), employ the same all-season
storage-gauge instrumentation as the target sites, and lie 43--47~mi south-southwest of the
target---upwind or crosswind of the prevailing southwesterly to westerly flow, and sufficiently
removed from the intended treatment area to minimize the likelihood of treatment influence. On
this basis the analysis adopts three pairs: La Sal Upper with Buckboard Flat, La Sal Lower with
Camp Jackson (assigned on elevation match, 8780 versus 8840~ft, and to avoid dependence of all
pairs on a single control gauge), and Gold Basin with Buckboard Flat. These are the pairs
presented in Figures~\ref{fig:july_boxplots} and~\ref{fig:season_boxplots}.
 
Two further groups of stations were examined and assigned supporting rather than primary roles.
Three SNOTEL sites in adjacent ranges---Columbine Pass (409), Lone Cone (589) and East Willow
Creek (461)---exhibit comparable correlations with the targets ($r = 0.51$--$0.83$) but lie
47--69~mi away across separate mountain ranges. At those separations, month-to-month agreement
with the target is unlikely to reflect the same individual storms, particularly for convective
precipitation; these sites are therefore reported as a regional reference on whether a given
month was broadly wet or dry across southeastern Utah and southwestern Colorado, not as
controls. Separately, the NWS Cooperative Observer Program (COOP) station at Arches NP HQS
provides a low-elevation perspective on the same events (Figure~\ref{fig:coop_boxplots}). COOP
sites correlate only moderately with La Sal Upper and La Sal Lower ($r = 0.54$--$0.63$) and are
used as a secondary consistency check for those two targets only; they lie several thousand feet
below the targets in semi-arid valley and desert-floor settings, employ manually read daily
gauges whose observation-time convention does not align with the automated SNOTEL record, and
are subject to observer-dependent gaps. The second COOP station used in previous reports,
Blanding, returned only two reporting days in July 2026 and is therefore omitted from this
reporting period.
 
Turning to the reporting period, the following observations are made.
 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item July 2026 was substantially wetter than June and above climatology at all three La Sal
        target sites. Calendar-month totals were approximately 4.7~in at La Sal Upper, 3.8~in at
        La Sal Lower and 4.3~in at Gold Basin, each above the corresponding July climatological
        mean and near the upper quartile of the 2012--2025 distribution.
  \item In contrast to June, the month provided substantial opportunity for evaluation:
        precipitation was recorded on eighteen days at one or more La Sal SNOTEL sites, with the
        largest daily accumulations on 19 July, and WETA was operating during the majority of
        these events.
  \item The target-minus-control differences for July 2026 were positive at La Sal Upper
        relative to Buckboard Flat ($+1.1$~in) and at Gold Basin relative to Buckboard Flat
        ($+0.7$~in), and negative at La Sal Lower relative to Camp Jackson ($-0.9$~in).
  \item Relative to the climatological difference distribution, the La Sal Upper pair sits near
        the upper end of its historical July range, whose mean is slightly negative; the Gold
        Basin pair is likewise positive against a slightly negative historical mean. The La Sal
        Lower pair remains within its interannual spread. The divergence in sign between pairs
        within the same range illustrates the sensitivity of single-pair monthly differences to
        convective storm placement, and no claim of enhancement is made for July in isolation.
  \item The Gold Basin comparison is shown for completeness, but its distribution should be
        interpreted with caution: the site entered service in October 2021, so only four paired
        July observations are available and its climatological range is correspondingly poorly
        constrained.
  \item Regional and low-elevation reference gauges confirm that July was wet across a wide
        area, but with pronounced spatial variability: Lone Cone recorded more July precipitation
        than any La Sal site, while Columbine Pass and East Willow Creek recorded substantially
        less, and the low-elevation COOP gauge at Arches NP HQS recorded very little.
        Comparisons involving Arches NP HQS should be treated as indicative only, as its July
        record is incomplete and the missing days coincide with the principal precipitation
        period of the month.
  \item Viewed across the warm season (Figure~\ref{fig:season_boxplots}), April--July 2026
        cumulative precipitation at La Sal Upper and Gold Basin exceeds that at the Abajo
        controls by a margin above the corresponding climatological cumulative difference, while
        La Sal Lower remains close to Camp Jackson. Aggregating across the season in this way
        reduces the influence of individual convective events relative to single-month
        comparisons.
  \item Cumulative analog-year estimates for July and for April--July 2026 are summarized in
        Table~\ref{tab:analog_years}. The estimates are positive at La Sal Upper on both the
        monthly and seasonal basis, and near zero to slightly negative at La Sal Lower. These
        central estimates rest on a short warm-season sample and should be treated as
        preliminary; confidence intervals will be reported alongside the additional statistical
        methods in the next reporting cycle.
\end{enumerate}
 
Three factors currently limit the sensitivity of this evaluation. First, the sample is short:
the longest paired record available for a primary pair is 13 warm-season years, and Gold Basin
has four. Second, warm-season precipitation in this region is dominated by convective events
whose spatial scale is smaller than the target--control separation, so a single control gauge
samples the storm field only imperfectly---as illustrated by the opposing signs obtained at the
Upper and Lower pairs this month. Third, month-to-month convective placement can reverse the
sign of a single-pair difference even when regional totals are elevated. To address these,
subsequent reports will (i) continue to aggregate differences across the full warm season rather
than relying on isolated months, (ii) incorporate gridded Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS)
quantitative precipitation estimates to characterize precipitation over the target area as a
whole rather than at points, and (iii) extend the gauge network through the additional
instrumentation planned for installation in the La Sal Mountains. It is further noted that
SNOTEL is not a homogenized climate-reference network \citep{fleming2023}; paired differencing
is used throughout to mitigate any instrument-era discontinuities common to both sites of a
pair.
