\section{Executive Summary}

This report provides a preliminary review of the Weather Enhancement Technology Array (WETA)
installed by Rain Enhancement Technologies (RET) on the Flat Iron Mesa, outside Moab, Utah.
WETA is a self-sufficient and ``off grid'' solution installed to provide enhancement of precipitation,
including snowfall during the cold season and rainfall during the warm season. For this report, the
warm season refers to the April--August operating period, with the present report focused specifically
on August 2026.

\subsection{Operational Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report covers 31 days from 08/01/2026 to 08/31/2026.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
    \item WETA operated on twenty-four days in August 2026.
    \item Precipitation was observed on six days in August at one or more La Sal SNOTEL sites.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item August 2026 was dry at the La Sal target sites relative to climatology. Target-minus-control
  differences were negative at all three primary pairs, though the La Sal Upper and La Sal Lower
  contrasts remain close to their historical August means. Sample size and convective spatial
  variability still limit definitive attribution for a single month.
  \item Given the absence of snowpack at the observation sites, this report focuses on liquid
  precipitation during the August operating period.
  \item The longest continuous operating period for WETA within August was 13 days, between
  08/18/2026 and 08/30/2026. Operations beginning 07/24/2026 carried into early August and
  ended at 1020 on 08/03/2026. WETA was shut down at 1256 on 08/30/2026 due to flood
  warnings.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actions and Improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report evaluates warm-season precipitation using methods consistent with those applied
  during the cold season, including comparisons between observations in the La Sal Mountains
  and selected reference stations from the NRCS SNOTEL network. Candidate stations were
  assessed based on historical correlation with the La Sal target sites, geographic relationship
  to the treatment area, elevation and climatic setting, and consistency of the available
  precipitation records. Primary controls are Abajo Mountains SNOTEL pairs
  (Buckboard Flat and Camp Jackson).
  \item Installation of additional instrumentation in the La Sal Mountains is planned for this summer,
  pending the necessary USDA Forest Service permitting and approvals.
  \item Methods of evaluation for warm-season precipitation are being tested presently, including
  incorporation of data from the Multi-Radar Multi-Sensor (MRMS) dataset and secondary
  consistency checks against NWS Cooperative Observer Program (COOP) stations.
  \item WETA operations will continue to target predicted precipitation events that are not considered
  adverse weather conditions.
  \item An automatic shut-off capability for lightning events occurring within a 6-mile radius of WETA
  is currently under development as a protection system for the WETA.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of Precipitation Enhancement}

Enhancement is evaluated by comparing accumulated precipitation at target sites in the La Sal
Mountains against control sites outside the treatment area, using records from the SNOTEL
locations. SNOTEL precipitation is measured with an all-season storage gauge, which reports
year-round and is therefore suitable for warm-season rainfall analysis. Candidate control sites were
drawn from the NRCS SNOTEL network and assessed on three joint criteria: correlation of
April--August monthly precipitation totals with the target, consistency of instrumentation with the
target gauges, and geographic position relative to the treatment area.

The Abajo Mountains provide the primary controls. Buckboard Flat and Camp Jackson are among
the most strongly correlated candidates for every La Sal target over the warm season, employ the
same all-season storage-gauge instrumentation as the target sites, and lie south-southwest of the
target. On this basis the analysis adopts three pairs: La Sal Upper with Buckboard Flat, La Sal
Lower with Camp Jackson, and Gold Basin with Buckboard Flat. These are the pairs presented in
Figures~\ref{fig:precip_month_clim} and~\ref{fig:precip_season}.

Turning to the reporting period, the following observations are made.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item August 2026 was dry relative to climatology at all three La Sal target sites. Calendar-month
  totals were approximately 0.4~in at La Sal Upper, 0.2~in at La Sal Lower, and 0.6~in at
  Gold Basin, each well below the corresponding August climatological mean.
  \item The Abajo control sites were also below climatology but wetter than the targets: Buckboard
  Flat and Camp Jackson each recorded approximately 1.1~in. The target-minus-control
  differences for August 2026 were therefore negative at La Sal Upper relative to Buckboard
  Flat ($-$0.7~in), at Gold Basin relative to Buckboard Flat ($-$0.5~in), and at La Sal Lower
  relative to Camp Jackson ($-$0.9~in).
  \item Relative to the climatological mean of the difference distribution, the La Sal Upper and La Sal
  Lower pairs remain close to their historical August means. The Gold Basin pair sits below
  its short-record climatological difference. No claim of enhancement is made for August in
  isolation.
  \item Viewed across the warm season (Figure~\ref{fig:precip_season}), July remains the wettest 2026
  month at the targets. April--August 2026 cumulative totals remain above the Abajo controls
  at La Sal Upper and Gold Basin, while La Sal Lower is below Camp Jackson.
  \item Cumulative analog-year estimates for August and for April--August 2026 are summarized in
  Table~\ref{tab:enhancement}. For La Sal Upper (Buckboard Flat control), the August analog-year
  enhancement is $+0.14$~in and the April--August seasonal analog-year enhancement is
  approximately $+4.1$~in. La Sal Lower is $+0.50$~in for August and near zero over the
  season. These seasonal central estimates remain preliminary given the short warm-season
  sample and convective spatial variability.
\end{enumerate}

Three factors currently limit the sensitivity of this evaluation. First, the sample is short: the
longest paired record available for a primary pair is on the order of a decade of warm-season
years, and Gold Basin has fewer overlapping years. Second, warm-season precipitation in this
region is dominated by convective events whose spatial scale is smaller than the
target--control separation, so a single control gauge samples the storm field only imperfectly.
Third, month-to-month convective placement can reverse the sign of a single-pair difference even
when regional totals are elevated.
