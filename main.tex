\section{Executive Summary}

This report provides a preliminary review of the Weather Enhancement Technology Array (WETA)
installed by Rain Enhancement Technologies (RET) on the Flat Iron Mesa, outside Moab, Utah.
WETA is a self-sufficient and ``off grid'' solution installed to provide enhancement of precipitation,
including snowfall during the cold season and rainfall during the warm season. For this report, the
warm season refers to the April--August operating period, with the present report focused specifically
on August 2026.

\subsection{Operational Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report covers 31 days from 08/01/2026 to 08/31/2026.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
    \item WETA operated on twenty-one days in August 2026.
    \item Precipitation was observed on eight days in August at one or more La Sal SNOTEL sites.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item August 2026 was markedly dry at the La Sal target sites. Target-minus-control differences were negative at all
  three primary pairs; the La Sal Upper and La Sal Lower contrasts remain within their
  interannual spread, while the Gold Basin contrast falls below its short-record mean. High spatial variability limits definitive attribution for a single month.
  \item Given the absence of snowpack at the observation sites, this report focuses on liquid
  precipitation during the August operating period.
  \item The longest continuous operating period for WETA within August was 13 days, between
  08/18/2026 and 08/30/2026. Operations beginning 07/24/2026 and carried into early August.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actions and Improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item This report evaluates warm-season precipitation using methods consistent with those applied
  during the cold season, including comparisons between observations in the La Sal Mountains
  and selected reference stations from the NRCS SNOTEL network. Candidate stations were
  assessed based on historical correlation with the La Sal target sites, geographic relationship
  to the treatment area, elevation and climatic setting, and consistency of the available
  precipitation records. Primary controls are Abajo Mountains SNOTEL pairs
  (Buckboard Flat and Camp Jackson).
  \item Installation of additional instrumentation in the La Sal Mountains remains planned,
  pending the necessary USDA Forest Service permitting and approvals.
 
  \item WETA operations will continue to target predicted precipitation events that are not considered
  adverse weather conditions.
  \item An automatic shut-off capability for lightning events occurring within a 6-mile radius of WETA
  is currently under development as a protection system for the WETA.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of Precipitation Enhancement}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation method is unchanged from previous reports. Accumulated precipitation at target
sites in the La Sal Mountains is compared against control sites outside the treatment area, using
SNOTEL all-season storage-gauge records and a climatology of paired
target--control differences spanning water years 2013--2025. The station-selection criteria and
the rationale for the adopted pairs are set out in full in the June 2026 report and are not
repeated here; in summary, the primary controls are the Abajo Mountains sites, giving three
pairs: La Sal Upper with Buckboard Flat, La Sal Lower with Camp Jackson, and Gold Basin with
Buckboard Flat (Figure~\ref{fig:precip_month_clim}). Three SNOTEL sites in adjacent ranges
(Columbine Pass, Lone Cone, East Willow Creek) are retained as a regional reference rather than
as controls.

In addition to the full-climatology comparison, an analog-year benchmark is computed for the two
primary pairs. For each pair, the five pre-treatment warm seasons (2013--2025) whose August
total at the target gauge is closest to the August 2026 total are selected as analog years, and
the enhancement estimate is the 2026 target-minus-control difference less the mean difference
over the analog years. The intent is to benchmark 2026 against climatologically similar (here,
dry-monsoon) seasons rather than against the full record; both targets independently select the
same five analog years (2015, 2017, 2019, 2020 and 2025), consistent with regionally coherent
dry summers.

Turning to the reporting period, the following observations are made.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item August 2026 was markedly dry relative to climatology at all three La Sal target sites.
        Calendar-month totals were 0.4~in at La Sal Upper, 0.2~in at La Sal Lower and 0.8~in at
        Gold Basin, in each case the lowest or near-lowest August total in the 2013--2025 record
        and well below the corresponding climatological means.
  \item Precipitation was recorded on six days at one or more La Sal SNOTEL sites, with the
        largest daily accumulations near 15 August. 
  \item The Abajo control sites were also below climatology, but substantially wetter than the
        targets: Buckboard Flat recorded 1.6~in and Camp Jackson 1.5~in. 
 
  \item Relative to the climatological difference distribution
        (Figure~\ref{fig:precip_month_clim}), the La Sal Upper and La Sal Lower pairs remain
        within their interannual spread, both sitting modestly below their historical August
        means, which are themselves negative. The Gold Basin pair falls below its short-record
        climatological difference. The reversal of sign at the Upper pair between July and August
        illustrates the sensitivity of single-pair monthly differences to convective storm
        placement, and no claim of enhancement is made for August in isolation.
  
  \item Viewed across the warm season (Figure~\ref{fig:precip_season}), July remains the wettest
        2026 month at the targets and August the driest. Aggregating across the season in this way reduces the influence of
        individual convective events relative to single-month comparisons.
  \item Analog-year enhancement estimates for August 2026 are presented in
        Figure~\ref{fig:analog_enhancement}: No claim of enhancement or suppression is made for August.
        Regression-counterfactual and Bayesian structural time-series estimates are not reported
        for this month: August was nearly rainless at the targets, leaving too few precipitation events for the daily
        statistical models to constrain a monthly estimate. Seasonal estimates,
        which aggregate across the full warm season, will be presented with the complete method
        suite in
        the next reporting cycle.
\end{enumerate}
